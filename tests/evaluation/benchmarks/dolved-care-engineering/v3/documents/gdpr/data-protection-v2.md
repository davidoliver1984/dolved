# Data Protection Policy

Document family: `family.gdpr.data-protection`
Version: 2.0
Effective: 1 January 2026
Synthetic engineering benchmark material

Personal information must be collected for a defined purpose, limited to what is necessary, kept accurate and retained only as long as required. Staff must lock paper records away and use approved systems for electronic records.

Suspected loss, misdirection or unauthorised disclosure must be reported through the privacy incident channel immediately and no later than four hours after discovery. Staff must not investigate by accessing another person's account.

## Responsibilities and lawful handling

Every employee is responsible for the information they create, receive and share. Managers design local processes so staff can meet this policy during normal work rather than relying on memory or informal workarounds. The Data Protection Officer advises on lawful basis, data-rights requests, high-risk processing and reportable breaches but does not take ownership away from operational teams.

Access is granted according to role and current need. A colleague's ability to open a record does not establish a legitimate reason to view it. Shared accounts are prohibited. Staff lock screens when leaving a device, keep authentication factors private and report unexpected access prompts to IT Security.

Special-category information about health, disability, ethnicity, beliefs, sexuality or trade-union membership requires additional care. It is recorded only where relevant to care, employment or another defined lawful purpose. Curiosity, convenience and a desire to build a “complete file” are not purposes.

## Sharing and communication

Before sharing personal information, staff confirm the recipient, purpose, minimum necessary content and secure channel. Email address auto-complete must be checked before sending. Large distributions use an approved mailing tool or blind-copy arrangement where recipients should not see one another.

Telephone callers are verified using proportionate questions that do not disclose the answer. Staff do not confirm that a person receives care merely because the caller knows a name or address. Where urgent information is necessary to prevent serious harm, staff share the minimum needed and document the reason rather than delaying solely because routine consent cannot be obtained.

Personal information is not copied into consumer messaging, personal email, removable media or unapproved AI services. Photographs taken for care or incident purposes use an approved device and system; copies are removed from the capture device after successful secure transfer.

## Accuracy, minimisation and retention

Records distinguish observed fact, professional judgement and information supplied by another person. Material errors are corrected promptly without concealing the audit history. A disputed opinion is not rewritten as fact; the challenge and outcome are recorded through the appropriate process.

Forms and reports collect only fields needed for their stated purpose. Reusing existing information is preferable to repeatedly asking a person for the same sensitive details, provided the reuse is compatible and accurate. Free-text fields must not become a place to store unrelated allegations, passwords or copied identity documents.

The records retention schedule defines routine deletion periods. A legal hold, safeguarding need or active complaint may suspend deletion for identified records. Staff must not keep private “just in case” copies when the authoritative record has reached disposal.

## Data rights

A request to access, correct, restrict or object to use of personal information is forwarded to the Privacy Team on the day received. No special wording is required. Staff preserve potentially relevant records and continue ordinary lawful work unless the Privacy Team gives a specific restriction.

Identity is verified proportionately. Excessive identification can itself create risk. Information about other people is reviewed before disclosure, but the presence of third-party material does not justify rejecting an entire request without analysis.

## New systems and changes

A data protection impact assessment begins before high-risk processing is approved, procured or piloted. Triggers include systematic monitoring, new uses of sensitive data, large-scale matching, automated decisions with significant effects and technology that people would not reasonably expect.

Contracts with processors define instructions, confidentiality, security, assistance with rights and incidents, deletion or return, audit information and control of sub-processors. Procurement completion does not by itself approve a new purpose for existing care or workforce data.

## Incident containment

After reporting, staff take safe reversible containment such as recalling a message, asking a known unintended recipient to preserve and delete it, or disconnecting a lost logged-in device through IT. They do not remotely erase evidence, contact unknown recipients repeatedly, promise a particular regulatory outcome or access accounts outside their authority.

The Privacy Team coordinates risk assessment with operational, safeguarding, clinical and security colleagues. Decisions record the people affected, data sensitivity, likely consequences, containment, notification reasoning and further prevention work.
