# Safe Administration of Medicines Policy

Document family: `family.medication.administration`
Version: 2.0
Effective: 1 May 2025
Synthetic engineering benchmark material

## Purpose and scope

This policy applies to all Alderbridge services and establishes the minimum
controls for receiving, preparing, administering and recording medicines. It
does not replace a person's prescription, care plan or PRN protocol. Staff must
work within their assessed competence and seek clinical advice whenever the
record, label and current instructions do not agree.

## Authorisation to administer

Only staff with current medicines training and a recorded competency assessment
may administer medicines without direct supervision. Agency staff must also
complete the local medicines orientation before their first medicines round.
The shift leader checks the allocation at the start of every shift.

## Seven checks

Immediately before administration, the member of staff must confirm the right person, medicine, dose, route, time, reason and documentation.
Identity must be
checked against the MAR and the person's agreed identifier; room number alone
is not an identifier. The medicine label must be legible, in date and consistent
with the current MAR instruction.

## Recording administration

The member of staff must sign the Medication Administration Record (MAR) immediately after observing that the person has taken the dose.
Staff must not pre-sign a MAR.
If the medicine is not given, the correct exception code and a contemporaneous note are required.

| Circumstance | MAR code | Immediate action |
|---|---|---|
| Person refuses | R | Record the reason offered and follow the care-plan escalation |
| Medicine unavailable | U | Inform the shift leader and arrange an urgent supply check |
| Clinical instruction to omit | O | Record the prescriber's name and instruction |
| Person away from service | A | Confirm the absence in the daily record |

## Missed, delayed or refused doses

Staff must not give a delayed dose until they have checked the medicine-specific
window or obtained clinical advice. A refused dose must be handled without
coercion. The shift leader must be informed before the end of the medicines
round, and urgent clinical advice is required where the medicine is time
critical or the care plan identifies a material risk.

## PRN medicines

PRN means medicine given when required. Before a PRN dose, staff must consult
the current protocol for indication, minimum interval and maximum daily dose.
The reason for administration and the observed outcome must be recorded. A PRN
medicine must not be offered merely because it appears on the MAR.

## Storage checks

Before administering a refrigerated medicine, staff must confirm that the
latest temperature record is within 2°C to 8°C.
If it is outside that range, the medicine must be quarantined and the medicines storage procedure followed; staff must not decide independently that the medicine remains usable.

## Controlled drugs

Where the controlled-drugs procedure requires a witness, both staff members
must complete the register at the time of administration. A later countersignature
does not satisfy the witness requirement.

## Incident escalation and audit

An unexplained MAR gap, incorrect dose, wrong person, wrong medicine or omitted
time-critical dose is a medication incident. Staff must first protect the person,
obtain clinical advice where needed, inform the shift leader and complete the
medication incident record. The registered manager arranges a monthly audit of
MAR records, stock balances and recurring exception codes.

## Preparation and interruption control

The administering worker prepares medicines for one person at a time and keeps the MAR available throughout the round. Medicines are not pre-poured for several people to save time. If preparation is interrupted before identity or medicine checks are complete, the worker returns to the beginning of the checking sequence.

The medicine trolley remains locked whenever the authorised worker is not in direct control of it. Keys are carried by the designated worker and are not left in a drawer, door or shared hiding place. Medicines taken away from the normal administration area remain labelled and under the worker's control until administered or returned to secure storage.

## Omissions, refusal and swallowing difficulty

When a person refuses a medicine, staff respect the decision, record the refusal using the correct MAR code and consider any immediate clinical risk. They do not disguise the medicine in food or drink unless the covert-administration process has been lawfully completed for that specific medicine.

A medicine is not recorded as administered until staff have reasonable evidence it was taken. If a tablet is dropped, spat out or later found, staff preserve what is safe to preserve, record the facts and seek advice before offering a replacement dose. Uncertainty is escalated; it is not resolved by guessing that the dose was or was not absorbed.

New swallowing difficulty triggers clinical review. Staff do not crush tablets, open capsules or mix medicines with food unless current medicine-specific advice authorises that route. A general statement that the person “prefers medicines crushed” is not sufficient.

## Time-critical and high-risk medicines

The care plan identifies time-critical medicines and the permitted administration window. Staff prioritise these medicines when a round is disrupted and seek clinical advice if the window is likely to be missed. The standard two-hour round does not silently redefine a narrower prescribed interval.

Insulin, anticoagulants, controlled drugs and other locally identified high-risk medicines receive the additional checks stated in their protocol. Additional checks complement rather than replace the ordinary rights of administration. A witness does not transfer accountability away from the administering worker.

## Changes and reconciliation

A medicine change is accepted only from an authorised prescriber or through the approved clinical communication route. Handwritten MAR amendments identify the medicine, instruction, source, date, time and staff member, with a second check where the procedure requires one. Staff do not alter a MAR from an ambiguous voicemail or a relative's recollection.

After hospital attendance, admission, discharge or another care transition, the receiving worker reconciles the prescription, discharge information, supplied medicines and current MAR. Differences are clarified before the next affected dose unless a clinician gives an explicit safe interim direction.

## Monitoring and escalation

Where a prescription or care plan requires observations, the worker confirms the current result before administration and records it in the designated place. A result copied from an earlier shift is not a current observation. Values outside the stated parameters are escalated before the medicine is given.

Suspected adverse effects are responded to according to urgency. Staff seek emergency help for severe breathing difficulty, collapse or another life-threatening reaction, then follow internal reporting. Less urgent concerns are reported promptly to the prescriber or clinical service and documented with onset, observations and action taken.

## End-of-round reconciliation

At the end of the round, the administering worker checks that every due MAR entry has an administration code, a documented reason for omission or an active escalation. Unexplained blank boxes are resolved before handover. The trolley, refrigerator and controlled-drug storage are secured, and any stock discrepancy is escalated under the relevant procedure.

Audit identifies learning, repeated omissions, late administration, unclear records and unsafe workarounds. It is not used to replace real-time supervision or to add retrospective signatures to incomplete records.
