# Annual Leave Policy

Document family: `family.hr.annual-leave`
Version: 2.0
Effective: 1 January 2025
Synthetic engineering benchmark material

Full-time employees receive 30 days of paid annual leave including public holidays. Requests for five or more consecutive working days must be submitted at least six weeks in advance.

Managers should respond within seven working days. Up to three unused days may be carried into the next leave year with written approval.

| Request length | Minimum notice |
| --- | --- |
| One or two working days | Seven calendar days |
| Three or four working days | Fourteen calendar days |
| Five or more working days | Six weeks |

## Planning and approval

Employees submit requests through the rota system and should not make non-refundable arrangements until approval is recorded. Managers consider safe staffing, already-approved leave, essential training and the employee's opportunity to take leave across the year. Requests are considered fairly in the order received, but order of receipt does not override a serious equality or wellbeing consideration.

A manager who cannot approve the requested dates discusses practical alternatives. Refusal must identify the operational reason; “service need” without an explanation is not sufficient. Approval belongs to the manager responsible for the affected rota and cannot be inferred from an unanswered message or a draft rota.

At least one half of annual entitlement should normally be planned by the end of September. Managers review low balances and unusually high remaining balances quarterly so leave is not concentrated at year end. Employees remain responsible for submitting requests; a review reminder is not automatic approval.

## Entitlement and recording

Part-time entitlement is calculated pro rata in hours so employees working different shift lengths receive an equivalent allowance. Public holidays are deducted only where they fall on a day or shift the employee would otherwise work. The HR system is the authoritative balance; local spreadsheets may help planning but must not create or remove entitlement.

When employment starts or ends part way through the leave year, entitlement is recalculated to the employment dates. Payroll may recover overtaken leave from final pay where the contract and law permit. Accrued untaken leave is paid on termination and is not converted into an informal extension of employment.

## Sickness and family leave

An employee who becomes unfit for work during booked leave may ask for the affected period to be treated as sickness absence. They must follow the sickness reporting procedure and provide evidence that would have been required had they been rostered to work. The manager does not reclassify leave solely because the weather, travel or personal plans were disappointing.

Annual leave continues to accrue during maternity, adoption and other statutory family leave. The employee and manager should plan how accrued leave will be taken around the family-leave period. Statutory carry-over rights take precedence over the ordinary three-day carry limit in this policy.

## Cancellation and emergencies

Approved leave is cancelled by Alderbridge only where a serious and unforeseen service risk cannot reasonably be managed another way. The senior manager records the reason, alternatives considered and any reasonable evidenced cancellation cost. Routine vacancies, delayed rota preparation or avoidable poor planning are not normally sufficient.

Employees who need time away for an emergency involving a dependant should use the emergency-dependants route rather than describing the absence as annual leave after the event. Other urgent personal circumstances may be considered under special leave. Choosing the correct route ensures that leave balances and workforce information remain accurate.

## Carry-over

Ordinary carry-over is limited to three days and needs written approval before the leave year closes. Approval records when the days will be used, normally within the first three months of the next year. Statutory carry-over caused by long-term sickness or family leave is recorded separately and is not reduced to the ordinary three-day limit.
